# Medical disclaimer

**Draft, version 2026-10-29.** Written by the founder with AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer or a clinician.

- **Good Habits is not a medical device.** It does not diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent any disease, and it is not a substitute for a doctor or registered dietitian.
- **No medication or supplement advice.** Good Habits never tells you to start, stop, or change a medication or a supplement dose. Anything it says about supplements is a topic to discuss with your clinician.
- **Talk with your doctor** about your lab results, and before you change your diet if you have a medical condition, take medication, or are pregnant or breastfeeding.
- **Results that need attention.** When a lab value is far outside the usual range, Good Habits asks you to contact your doctor promptly instead of interpreting it. If you have symptoms or feel unwell, contact your doctor. In an emergency, call 911.
- **AI-generated content.** Explanations, meal plans, and chat answers are generated by AI and can be wrong. Nutrition numbers come from a food database, not from AI.
- **Safety limits.** Calorie targets never go below conventional minimums, weight loss is capped at 1 percent of body weight a week, and some features are turned off for pregnancy, eating disorders, dialysis, transplant, and people under 18. These limits were reviewed by the founder with AI assistance, not independently by a clinician.
- **If you are struggling with eating,** please talk with your doctor or a licensed eating disorder specialist. If you are in crisis in the US, call or text 988.
